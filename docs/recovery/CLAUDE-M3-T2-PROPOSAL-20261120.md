- **My onset detector would have measured the splice edge.** In that same file the first 5 ms of label 0 already reaches 0.0286 against a segment peak of 0.0287, a hard step from digital silence. v1's "onset" windows would have been label-relative windows with a spurious onset.

## 2. New verified facts about the onset

- The original source recordings are still on disk. Each `build/physical-capture/pitch-corrected/string-NN-manifest.json` records `source_wave` (under `~/Development/Experiments/PreSonus Revelator | UniCont/`), its `source_wave_sha256`, and each observation's `source_start_sample`. For the three manifests I compared (strings 1, 3, 8) the recorded hashes equal the pins in `tests/fixtures/m3_physical_a440/manifest-v1.json`. I did not re-hash the 118 to 178 MB originals.
- **The pluck is well before the label start.** In string 3's original recording I took 10 ms RMS envelopes from 4 s before to 1 s after each label start and located the largest 10 ms rise. Relative to the label start it falls at a median of -380 ms in pass 1 (10th to 90th percentile -400 to -334 ms, 25 labels) and -380 ms in pass 2 (25 labels); in pass 3 the median is -325 ms (20 labels, 15% within 50 ms of the label start). The pluck is more than 50 ms before the label start for 92%, 100% and 80% of labels in passes 1 to 3. Before the label start the level is already steady: for the first ten pass-1 labels the RMS 100 ms before the start is 0.9 to 1.15 times the level just after it.
- Consequences, with the limits below: (a) the first 160 ms of a label is roughly 380 to 540 ms after the pick, so it is early sustain, which matches Codex's "early labeled segment" wording; (b) the retained 24000-sample pre-roll (250 ms) cannot contain the onset when the onset is about 380 ms earlier, so a nonzero prefix would not make it an onset experiment either; (c) the isolated-note replay corpus (`raw.wav`) never contains a pick attack, only the spliced sustain; (d) a genuine onset study has to read the original source recordings.
- Limits: one string (string 3), a largest-rise heuristic rather than a verified onset detector, and one label per note. The first label of the file had its largest rise at -3100 ms, so a few labels are outliers; one pass-1 label (note 57) has its pluck 70 ms after the label start. Treat the -380 ms as a strong lead to confirm on the other strings, not a measured constant, and do not generalize it beyond string 3 (it is one string, one heuristic, and in pass 3 the spread is wider). Later reading of the repo's own READMEs (`build/physical-capture/string-08/README.md`: "beginning 450 ms after each discrete attack"; `string-04/README.md`: "Label windows exclude each detected attack and terminal decay") shows the labels were designed as a fixed offset after a *detected* attack, which is consistent with a lead of a few hundred ms. That supports the design, not any individual clip: nothing in the repo verifies the offset per clip, and section 12 shows a pass-3 label can sit on the wrong note.

## 3. Review of `tools/portable_short_window_identity.py` (read-only, as of 02:50 local)

2. **A nonzero prefix cannot verify an onset (updated after GHA 44723230792).** The report's `input_provenance` says the clips came from the original physical isolated recordings, and 25 of 28 clips have a fully nonzero 15360-sample prefix (the other three have 1, 1 and 14 zero samples). 3. **Window versus fundamental.** An 80 ms Hann window with bands of plus or minus 0.35 times the fundamental is fine at note52 (165 Hz). 4. **`width` may track level.** It is the energy-weighted frequency spread inside a fixed band, so the noise floor at the band edges enters it. Check it against a level or noise-floor covariate before reading it as peak bandwidth. This is a thing to test, not a finding.

## 4. A feature difference I can still offer: body decay slopes (lower priority than section 9)

A feature the rejected 750 ms detuning screen and the 160 ms shape/change/width runner do not cover, and that needs no onset claim: the decay rate of each of partials 2 to 6 relative to partial 1, measured inside the labeled body.

- **Data:** the labeled intervals are 220800 samples (2.3 s), and the remote asset's clips contain the whole interval, so no new upload is needed for the note52 clips.
- **Definition (add as a feature group in Codex's runner, not a second runner):** over 0.10 s to 1.50 s of the labeled interval, in 80 ms windows every 40 ms, take the log band power of harmonics 1 to 6 as the runner already computes it; fit a least-squares slope per harmonic; the feature is slope(h) minus slope(1) for h = 2 to 6 (five numbers). Wound and plain strings of different gauge damp upper partials differently, so this is a physical string-identity quantity the runtime's resonator bank does not hold today (I did not check the calibration image for a decay field; `calibration_state_image.cpp` lists cents, six amplitudes, six detunings, confidence, count, quality).
- **Predicted limits, so a miss is interpretable:** (a) in a chord the slope of a partial that overlaps another note's partial is contaminated, so it needs the overlap mask from my T2b; (b) a slope needs at least 0.5 s, so it cannot choose the lane when a note is born. At best it supports correcting or holding a lane afterwards, which is relevant to the label54 attack growth (82 to 89), not to the first decision; (c) same-session pass holdout, same count-and-recall reporting as the other groups.

## 9. Early log shape versus the runtime's learned-profile semantics (read-only, answers Codex's request after GHA 44723230792)

Method: vector arithmetic only, on two small saved files (the 28 shape vectors in `build/github-results/short-window-identity-44723230792/short-window-identity/report.json` and the note52 points in `build/github-results/reserved-assignment-costs-44417227738/note52-calibration-points.json`) and a read of `polyphonic_pitch_detector.cpp` (`calibration_similarity_from_energy_profile`, about line 11835). No audio was read, nothing was dispatched. Scripts: scratchpad `shape_vs_runtime.py` and `replicate_and_probe.py` (not in the repo; they run in a second on the saved JSON and I can paste them if you want them reviewed).

**Replication.** Rebuilding your leave-one-pass-out nearest-centroid on the report's own vectors gives 8/8 for lanes 0/1 (shape) and 20/28 for six lanes, identical to your report.

**What the runtime compares.** `observed = corrected_harmonic_energy[h] / sum`, a linear energy share; `learned = harmonic_profile_q15[h] / 32767`, also a linear share; similarity is the cosine of the two, times 1 for measured points, times `0.75 + 0.25 * confidence`, and the assignment weight is 512. Your probe uses mean-centred log10 band power, scaled per component and compared by squared distance. These are different geometries.

**The stored note52 templates are almost one-hot.** Shares of the bank profiles (factory bank `b7025b4f...`): lane0 `[1, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0]`; lane1 `[0.99957, 3.97e-4, 0, 0, 0, 0]`; lane2 `[0.9974, 2.44e-3, 1.5e-4, 0, 0, 0]`; lane3 `[0.944, 0.055, 3e-4, 1e-4, 3e-5, 0]`; lanes 4/5 carry real upper partials. One q15 step is 3.05e-5. The measured early shapes in your report, converted to shares, agree with the bank to within the clip-to-clip spread for lanes 1 and 2 (h2/h1 power ratio, clips versus bank: lane1 2.3e-4 to 5.9e-4 versus 4.0e-4; lane2 6e-4 to 3e-3 versus 2.4e-3), and lane0's measured h2/h1 of 1.2e-5 to 6.2e-5 in three clips (the fourth is 5.1e-4) lies within about two quantization steps, which is consistent with the stored lane0 profile being exactly zero above h1. So the bank is not wrong about the audio; it records the part that matters in units that cannot show it. The two estimators differ (an FFT band sum on a Hann window versus the detector's resonator cells), so I call this agreement of orders of magnitude, not equality.

**What the cosine does with that.** Applying the runtime formula to the eight lane0/lane1 clips, `cos(lane0) - cos(lane1)` is between 1e-8 and 1.5e-7 for seven of them, that is 0.00001 to 0.00008 cost units after the 512 weight, against 14.485 from the confidence term. The sign is right for seven of eight clips (6 of the 7 ordinary clips; the eighth is the odd clip below), so the information is present in the linear cosine too, five to seven orders of magnitude below any other term, and in a live tick it would sit under any estimator noise. This tightens my earlier statement: the 0.203-unit figure was only an upper bound from template distance; the realized shape effect on these clips is at most 8e-5 (ordinary clips).

**Isolated-note analogue of the target failure.** With only the profile term, on your 28 clips: six-lane argmax is 12/28 with the pure cosine and 9/28 after the confidence weight. Weighting moves all three ordinary lane1 clips (all correct under the pure cosine) and all five ordinary lane2 clips (two correct under the pure cosine) to lane0, which is the lane0-over-lane1 mechanism of the chord, without a chord. Caveats: profile term alone with no fret, open, cents or retention terms; the bank was probably built from the same recordings, so this is not held out; different estimator. It is not a runtime prediction.

**Two odd clips to treat before reporting 8/8.** `s1-n52-attack3` (lane1, pass 3) and `s2-n52-attack5` (lane2, pass 3) have h2 above h1 (log10 h2/h1 of +0.43 and +2.27; every other clip is -2.5 or lower for lanes 0 to 2) and h1 centred values of 0.88 and 0.37 against 4 to 5.6 for their lane neighbours. Both are the later takes in pass 3. The z-scored squared distance of `s1-n52-attack3` is 71.5 to lane0 and 60.7 to lane1, against at most 4.8 for every other held-out pair clip, so its "correct" is a 15% margin between two far distances, not separation. Without that clip and without it in training the pair result is 7/7. Codex's decision, which I accept: keep all 28 clips and both unusual clips in every denominator and headline 8/8 for the pair; 7/7 is not stronger evidence and should not be presented as such, at most as an exploratory note. An outlier or eligibility rule still has to be fixed before looking at labels, not after. The reason these takes differ is now partly answered in section 12 (pass 3 is a separate checkpoint protocol; s0 a3 and s4 a2 carry the lane's open-string spectrum; s2 a5 matches the previous row's note); `s1-n52-attack3` is still unexplained.

**What this suggests, as hypotheses to test.** (1) Representation: linear-share cosine with one-hot-quantized templates cannot register lane0/1/2 differences that are decades apart in the upper partials. (2) Floor: whether the live resonator cells ever report h2 to h6 shares as low as 1e-5 to 1e-3 of h1 in a clean isolated note52 is unknown to me; if their floor is higher, the information your offline estimator sees is not available at runtime at all, and a log-domain term would just amplify leakage. (3) Provenance: how the bank profile was computed (window, normalization, whether upper partials were floored) is not something I have read.

**The material difference, stated for the proposal.** Nothing new is needed in the measurement to see the separation: your fixed 160 ms log shape already carries it, and my bandwidth and decay suggestions are secondary. The unmeasured link is runtime-side. Proposed bounded follow-up, owned by you: in a replay of the already staged note52 clips, record the six `corrected_harmonic_energy` values per decision tick within the first 160 ms (existing diagnostic output or one added trace field), then run your same evaluator on those runtime shares, side by side with the cosine. That answers hypothesis (2) and shows whether a log-domain template would survive the runtime's own estimator. Nothing in this selects or authorizes a DSP change.
