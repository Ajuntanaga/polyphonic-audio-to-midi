## Answer first

**On real audio the adapted model's release, measured in model frame time, is only about one frame later than the original's (median +11.6 ms over 120 paired ends), and holds past the label end for a median of 31 ms (original 16 ms). What differs is presence at the label end: the adapted output is still above the 0.3 threshold at 62% of label ends, the original at 29%.** The extra output the adapted run puts in the label gaps is that presence converted into about 108 ms of delivery lag. Applying my earlier "run still alive 102 ms after the label end" definition to these ONNX arms gives 58.2% adapted and 24.8% unmodified (earlier TensorFlow figures 59.2% and 21.5%), and the tape's presence at the end agrees with that classification at 95.6% and 94.9% of ends. So the earlier tail figure is a level-at-the-end effect shifted by delivery lag, and not evidence that the adapted model falls more slowly.

Registered predictions: H1, H2, H3 and C1 miss, H4 not evaluated; the details below, including two misses that are near-misses on a magnitude bar while the count is lopsided in the predicted direction. The mapping check passed for all four arm-corpus pairs.

The code gives the mechanism: a readout at frame 164 is delivered when the window ends, 1860 model samples (84.4 ms) after the frame's nominal time, and the decoder confirms a release on the third frame (+23.2 ms), so an event-clock run ends about 108 ms after its frame-time release. A note the model still sees at the label end therefore reaches the end of a 102 ms gap on the event clock with a margin of only a few milliseconds, and it does in 248 of the 266 adapted-detected ends and 104 of the 124 original-detected ends. The cross-tab does not by itself separate lag from other causes; the arithmetic does.

6. **Note length.** Split at the 748 ms median: presence at the end original 63 and 61, adapted 140 and 126 (short half, long half). No length dependence.

## What this does and does not say

- It says the earlier statement that the adapted model "holds released notes longer" (tail medians 325 against 133 ms on long gaps, 59.2% against 21.5% alive at the gap end) is mostly the model sitting above the threshold at the label end more often, then carried across the gap by the ~108 ms delivery lag that both arms share. In frame time the adapted hold is one frame later and its median tail is 31 ms. I did not re-derive the long-gap medians from the tapes, so the 325 and 133 ms figures stay what they were: event-clock values that include the lag.
- It says a decay-shaped teacher term, or boundary views that teach an abrupt end, would be aimed at a hold that is short on real audio. The synthetic probe found no longer hold either. Neither probe supports the boundary-fit draft; it should be judged on its own gates, which Codex already says.
- It says the levers on gap output are the delivery lag and the presence at the end, not the fall: lag (readout position, the decoder's 3-frame release confirmation) is not something a weight fit changes.
- It does not say whether the presence at the 145 adapted-only ends is right. The original had already dropped those notes by the end of the label; the adapted keeps them for the 30 ms median past it. If those strings were still sounding (post-label audibility, which Codex keeps open) this is the adapted model being right and the labels ending early; if they had been damped it is stickiness. Tapes cannot separate these, because they contain no audio level.
- Caveats: one recording for the verdicts (ends within a take are not independent; p-values are descriptive), absolute delays carry up to one frame of clock uncertainty (cancels in the paired differences), plateau and release use the 0.3 decoder threshold only, and the `extended-middle` read has six ends.
